# Methods — exploratory single-participant EEG comparison

## Design and source data
Three EDF+ recordings from one participant in one session were examined in the fixed order eyes open (EO), eyes closed (EC), and TCF intervention. According to the data provider, the participant's eyes were closed throughout TCF and the intervention was active throughout that file. TCF is the supplied experimental-condition label; it is not an independently verified exposure measurement. EC was the principal comparison condition, with EO–EC used as a secondary descriptive comparison. The analysis was developed after inspecting these data and was not preregistered.

The exported files contained 24 physiological signal channels sampled at 256 Hz, plus an EDF annotation channel. File durations were 362 s (EO), 377 s (EC), and 284 s (TCF). Signal labels indicated a CPz reference. This reference was retained without re-referencing; the original acquisition reference and any earlier transformations require confirmation from the recording team. The electrode labels were Fp1, Fp2, F7, F3, Fz, F4, F8, C3, Cz, C4, P3, Pz, P4, O1, O2, T7, T8, P7, P8, F9, F10, M1, M2, and Oz. Hardware filters, prior preprocessing, device model, electrode impedances, and digitized electrode coordinates were not available in the supplied headers. No dedicated EOG or ECG channel was labelled. Annotations indicated recording start and the respective task, with labelled durations approximately 361.443, 376.944 and 283.669 s; complete four-second epochs and boundary exclusions kept the primary analysis within these intervals.

## Import and quality assessment
EDF signal integers were converted into microvolts using each channel's physical and digital calibration extrema: x = (digital − digital_min) × (physical_max − physical_min)/(digital_max − digital_min) + physical_min. The reader checks format, file length, physical units, sample counts, and common sampling rate. It supports continuous standard 16-bit EDF/EDF+C and rejects EDF+D; it is not a general-purpose BDF or discontinuous-EDF reader. Annotation bytes were inspected separately and were not treated as EEG.

Channel quality was summarized using within-epoch standard deviation and peak-to-peak amplitude in contiguous, nonoverlapping four-second epochs. T8 was effectively flat across all conditions and F3 showed persistent high-amplitude fluctuations (median four-second peak-to-peak amplitudes approximately 422, 265 and 296 µV for EO, EC and TCF). Both were excluded from every condition. M1 and M2 were excluded from the scalp-electrode summaries because they are mastoid labels. These exclusions were selected after quality inspection, not inferred as neurological findings. Overview traces were visually inspected; exhaustive manual artifact scoring was not performed.

The main analysis removed the first and last complete four-second epochs of each file; incomplete trailing samples were discarded. For the 20-channel scalp summary, an epoch was rejected if any included channel had peak-to-peak amplitude >150 µV, sample standard deviation <0.1 µV, or an adjacent-sample step >50 µV. A posterior summary used P3, Pz, P4, P7, P8, O1, Oz and O2, with rejection based on the same rules across those eight channels only. Consequently, the two summaries use different accepted time segments. Every rejection decision is recorded in `results/epoch_quality.csv`. Accepted means passing these numerical rules, not proven artifact-free. Channels were not interpolated and ICA was not applied.

## Spectral estimation
No additional high-pass, low-pass or notch filter was applied. Each accepted epoch was demeaned, multiplied by a periodic Hann window and transformed with a 1,024-point FFT. A one-sided power spectral density (PSD; µV²/Hz) was computed by dividing squared FFT magnitude by sampling rate times the sum of squared window weights, doubling positive-frequency bins except Nyquist. Frequency spacing was 0.25 Hz. Modified periodograms were averaged arithmetically across accepted epochs within each channel. This is an average of windowed, nonoverlapping segment periodograms, without overlap or zero-padding. Spectra through Nyquist are exported to permit inspection of electrical-frequency contamination; quantitative band summaries are restricted to 1–30 Hz. No claim is made that line noise or its possible leakage was eliminated.

Absolute band power was calculated by summing PSD bins multiplied by 0.25 Hz over delta [1,4), theta [4,8), alpha [8,13), and beta [13,30) Hz. Brackets indicate inclusion of the lower boundary and exclusion of the upper boundary. These half-open definitions avoid counting boundary bins twice. Relative band power was 100 × band power / total power over [1,30) Hz for each channel. Regional summaries are the arithmetic means of channel absolute powers and, separately, channel relative percentages; the latter is not a ratio of pooled regional powers. No alpha-peak frequency, source localization or connectivity inference was made. Measured individual electrode coordinates were unavailable, so scalp interpolation maps were not generated.

## Comparisons and sensitivity analyses
TCF–EC and EC–EO contrasts were expressed as percentage changes in absolute band power, 100 × (comparison/baseline − 1), and decibels, 10 log10(comparison/baseline). Relative-power changes were expressed in percentage points. The analysis was repeated with a 100 µV peak-to-peak threshold, with all conditions restricted to their first 280 s before excluding the first and last complete epoch, and with boundary epochs included while retaining artifact rejection. The 280 s analysis matches observation-window duration, not accepted data quantity. Other quality thresholds and the channel sets were held fixed.

There was one biological participant, one block per condition, no sham block, no repeated or randomized condition order, and no independent control group. No p-values, population confidence intervals, or significance claims were calculated. Epochs and electrodes were not treated as independent participant replicates. Sensitivity analyses describe dependence on analysis choices and do not establish causal validity.

## Software and reproducibility
The delivered numerical results and figures were generated using `validation/analyze_python.py` with NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib; exact runtime versions are recorded in `results/python_versions.json`. The package also includes a dependency-free base-R implementation, `R/analyze.R`, using binary EDF import and `stats::mvfft`. **R was unavailable in the execution environment, so the R implementation has not been executed here.** It must be run and checked with `validation/validate_R.R` before describing these results as R-generated. A known-amplitude sinusoid, an independent FFT normalization calculation, and raw-data integrity checks were used to verify the executed pipeline. See `validation/validation_results.json`.

## Limits on interpretation and publication
Without additional filtering or exhaustive artifact correction, low-frequency power can include drift, eye activity and movement, and beta can include muscle activity. An absence of a clear eyes-closed alpha enhancement warrants review of acquisition, state and data quality; it does not diagnose abnormality. Fixed ordering prevents separating TCF from time-on-task, fatigue, expectancy, attention and other session changes. These recordings can describe condition-associated differences but cannot demonstrate the existence, mechanism, efficacy or causality of a consciousness field.

Before journal submission, add verified participant characteristics, inclusion criteria, consent and ethics information, acquisition equipment/settings, environmental conditions, exact intervention instructions, practitioner training (if applicable), and any original preprocessing. Do not invent or infer those details. Header-redacted copies are provided for repository preparation; participant consent and authority to publicly share the EEG must be established separately. Original files were not changed.
